This compares the European specs of the BYD Sealion 7 82.5 kWh RWD Comfort and the Tesla Model Y RWD, both model year 2026. They are midsize rear-wheel-drive SUVs with almost equal dimensions (4,830 mm long for the BYD and 4,794 mm for the Tesla), but with opposite approaches: one bets on a big battery and full equipment, the other on efficiency.

The Sealion 7's battery is 82.5 kWh and the Model Y's, 60 kWh. Even so, the Tesla's WLTP range is higher: 525 km (326 miles) against 482 km (300 miles). The reason lies in consumption, 13.4 kWh per 100 km for the Model Y and 19.9 kWh for the Sealion 7. The BYD weighs 2,225 kg, 319 kg more than the Tesla, and that mass is paid for over every kilometer. Frequent highway drivers will use more energy per kilometer in the BYD.

Fast charging also favors the Tesla: it accepts 175 kW and goes from 10 to 80% in 24 minutes, while the Sealion 7 accepts 150 kW and takes 32 minutes, though its battery is bigger and has more energy to fill. On a long trip, those eight minutes show up at every stop.

The BYD answers with power and acceleration, at 230 kW (308 hp) and 6.7 s from 0 to 100 km/h, against 220 kW (295 hp) and 7.2 s for the Tesla. The gap is modest and shows more when overtaking than in daily use.

The trunk tips things toward the Tesla: 835 liters against 520 for the Sealion 7, and 117 liters in the front trunk against 58. For a family traveling with luggage, that gap matters more than any acceleration figure.

On equipment, the Sealion 7 is the more complete according to the spec sheets. It has a 360° camera, blind spot monitor, panoramic roof, ventilated seats, Android Auto, Apple CarPlay and V2L to power external devices. On the Model Y's spec sheet, the 360° camera and blind spot monitor have no data; panoramic roof, ventilated seats, Android Auto, Apple CarPlay and V2L are listed as not included, as is lane keeping assist. On warranty, BYD gives 6 years or 150,000 km (93,000 miles) on the car and 250,000 km (155,000 miles) on the battery, against 4 years or 80,000 km (50,000 miles) and 160,000 km (99,000 miles) for the Tesla.

The Model Y suits people who travel a lot and value efficiency, faster charging and a big trunk. The Sealion 7 suits people who use the car mainly in the city and on medium trips, and want ventilated seats, a 360° camera and a better warranty. This comparison does not take price into account.
