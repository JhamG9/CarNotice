"Buy an EV and you get half back in taxes" is one of the most shared lines in Colombian videos about electric cars. It is not true. What exists is a **deduction** from income tax, and there is a huge difference between deducting and getting money back.

## What the rule says

Article 11 of **Law 1715 of 2014** allows income tax filers to deduct up to **50% of the value of an investment** in efficient energy management. Since 2025, the DIAN, Colombia's tax authority, has said in several opinions that buying an EV or a hybrid (HEV or PHEV) for personal use counts as that investment, as long as it has a **certificate from the UPME**, the country's energy planning unit.

The main conditions:

- The deduction can be spread over **up to 15 years**.
- Each year it cannot exceed **50% of the taxpayer's net income**.
- For individuals, the DIAN clarified that it is also subject to the general limit in **section 3 of article 336** of the Tax Code.
- According to DIAN Letter 178 of 2026, it applies from the tax year after the investment enters operation. How that date is set depends on each case: confirm it with your accountant.

## The cap almost nobody mentions

Section 3 of article 336 says that an individual's exempt income and deductions combined cannot exceed **40% of net income**, with a maximum of **1,340 UVT a year**. In 2026, with the UVT (the tax value unit) at COP 52,374, that maximum equals **COP 70,181,160**.

Other things already use that cap: the 25% exempt portion of salary, dependents, mortgage interest and prepaid health care. If your return already reaches the limit, the car deduction **will not lower your tax by a single peso** that year.

## An example with numbers

Suppose an employee with net income of COP 150 million a year buys a COP 100 million EV with a UPME certificate:

| Step | Value |
|---|---|
| Possible deduction for the car (50%) | COP 50 million, which can be spread over several years |
| Limit of 40% of net income | COP 60 million |
| UVT cap for 2026 | COP 70.2 million |
| What exempt salary income and other deductions already use | Depends on each return |

If your other benefits already add up to, say, COP 40 million, you have about COP 20 million of room that year. With a marginal rate near 28%, the tax saving would be about **COP 5.6 million**, not COP 50 million. You can try to use the rest of the deduction in later years, as long as the 15 years have not run out and there is room under the cap.

> The clear message: the deduction is a reduction of the taxable base, not a refund. What it is worth depends on your income, your rate and how much room your other benefits leave you.

## Who it is worth it for

- **Self-employed people and investors** with little exempt salary income, who have more room under the cap.
- **People with high incomes**, whose marginal rate is higher.
- **Anyone buying in the name of a company** that files as a legal entity, because the article 336 limit is for individuals. There, an accountant's review is essential.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I have to file income tax to get the benefit?

Yes. The deduction only exists within the income tax return. If you are not required to file, you cannot use it.

### Where is it recorded on the return?

The DIAN indicates that it goes in the income section, under "Other attributable deductions", with the limits in article 11 of Law 1715 and section 3 of article 336.

### Can I also request the VAT refund?

Yes, they are two different benefits. The condition is that the VAT refunded to you is not also used as a cost or an income tax deduction.

### Does it apply to hybrids?

According to the DIAN, it applies to HEV and PHEV hybrids with a UPME certificate, not to mild hybrids that do not meet the technical conditions.
