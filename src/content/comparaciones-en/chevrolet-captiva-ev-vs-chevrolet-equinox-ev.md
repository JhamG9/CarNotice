This comparison puts the entry versions sold in Colombia head to head: the Chevrolet Captiva EV in its single trim (model year 2026) and the Chevrolet Equinox EV LT AWD (2025). Both are midsize electric SUVs, but they start from different ideas: the Captiva has one motor and front-wheel drive, the Equinox has two motors and all-wheel drive.

### Battery, range and charging

The biggest difference is the energy on board. The Equinox carries 89 kWh and is rated at 552 km (343 miles) on the WLTP cycle; the Captiva carries 60 kWh and is rated at 318 km (198 miles) on the same cycle. For anyone traveling between cities, that 234 km (145 mile) margin means one less charging stop on long trips. At 15.4 kWh per 100 km, the Equinox is also the only one of the two with a consumption figure on its spec sheet, so the Captiva's cannot be checked against it.

For fast charging, the Equinox reaches 150 kW and the Captiva 100 kW. The measurements are not identical: the Captiva goes from 30 to 80% in 20 minutes and the Equinox from 10 to 80% in 50 minutes, a wider stretch that cannot be matched minute for minute. Only the Equinox declares AC charging, at 11.5 kW.

### Power and size

The Equinox delivers 212 kW (284 hp) and goes from 0 to 100 km/h in 6.1 s. The Captiva delivers 149.9 kW (201 hp) and takes 8.9 s. With two motors and a bigger battery, the Equinox is heavier: 2,322 kg against 1,780 kg. It is also larger, at 4,840 mm long with a 748-liter trunk against 4,740 mm and 532 liters, a volume you notice with suitcases or strollers.

### Safety and equipment

The Equinox declares 8 airbags and blind spot warning; the Captiva, 6 airbags and no such assistant. Both have autonomous braking, adaptive cruise, lane keeping and a 360° camera. The Equinox has a 17.7-inch screen, with heated and ventilated seats, but its spec sheet says it has neither Android Auto nor Apple CarPlay. The Captiva, with a 15.6-inch screen, has both, which counts for people who live on their phone in the car.

### Who each one suits

The Equinox EV suits people who travel often, regularly fill the trunk or want all-wheel drive and more power. The Captiva EV makes sense for mostly urban use with occasional highway trips, with less weight and direct phone connection. This comparison does not take price into account.
