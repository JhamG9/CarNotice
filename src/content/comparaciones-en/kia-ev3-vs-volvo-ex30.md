This compares the European specs of the Kia EV3 Standard Range and the Volvo EX30 P3, two small single-motor SUVs with different drive layouts: front-wheel drive on the Kia, rear-wheel drive on the Volvo.

The EV3 is ahead in almost everything measured in distance. It has a 58.3 kWh NMC battery against the EX30's 51 kWh LFP, and is rated at 436 km (271 miles) WLTP against 339 km (211 miles): almost 100 km (62 miles) more per charge. It also uses less energy, 14.9 kWh per 100 km against 17 kWh. Anyone covering more than 300 km (186 miles) in a day in the EX30 will have to stop and charge much sooner.

Power follows the same pattern: 150 kW (201 hp) and 283 Nm in the Kia, 110 kW (148 hp) and 343 Nm in the Volvo. From 0 to 100 km/h, the EV3 takes 7.5 s and the EX30, 8.6 s. The Volvo has more torque, but delivers it with less power.

### Where the Volvo answers

Fast charging is a point in the EX30's favor. It accepts up to 134 kW and goes from 10 to 80% in 26 minutes; the EV3 reaches 100 kW and needs 30. That is 4 minutes less per stop. It also has a blind spot monitor, which the Kia does not include in this trim. In exchange, it lacks adaptive cruise control, which the EV3 offers as standard.

### Space and warranty

The EV3 has more trunk space: 460 liters plus 25 under the hood, against 318 liters and 7 for the EX30. In dimensions, the Kia is slightly longer (4,300 mm against 4,233 mm) and wider (1,850 mm against 1,837 mm), with a slightly longer wheelbase, 2,680 mm against 2,650 mm.

The general warranty is where they differ most: 7 years or 150,000 km (93,000 miles) for the Kia against 3 years or 100,000 km (62,000 miles) for the Volvo. The battery warranty is the same, 8 years or 160,000 km (99,000 miles), so the difference only affects the rest of the car.

This comparison does not take price into account. The EV3 suits people who need more range, trunk space and years of coverage. The EX30 suits people who make short trips, recharge often at fast chargers and want the blind spot monitor.
