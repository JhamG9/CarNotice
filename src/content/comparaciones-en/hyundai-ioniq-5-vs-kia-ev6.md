This compares the European specs of the entry trims: the Hyundai IONIQ 5 63 kWh RWD and the Kia EV6 Standard Range 2WD. The figures change in other markets, where batteries and equipment are not the same.

On this data, the two cars are almost twins inside. Both carry a 63 kWh NMC battery and a 125 kW (168 hp), 350 Nm rear motor, and both charge on DC at up to 195 kW: going from 10 to 80% takes them 18 minutes. On a long trip, that means the same stop at the charger, with no advantage for either.

The difference appears in what they do with that battery. The IONIQ 5 is rated at 440 km (273 miles) WLTP and 15.6 kWh per 100 km; the EV6, 428 km (266 miles) and 16.4 kWh. That is a 12 km (7 mile) margin in the Hyundai's favor, explained by the Kia's higher consumption with the same stored energy. There is also a small gap in acceleration: 8.5 s from 0 to 100 km/h against 8.7 s, which nobody will feel in traffic.

### Space and shape

The IONIQ 5 is shorter (4,655 mm against 4,695 mm) but has a longer wheelbase: 3,000 mm against 2,900 mm. That difference usually translates into more legroom for rear passengers, although the spec sheet does not measure it. What is measured is the trunk: 520 liters against 490, plus 57 liters under the hood against 52 for the Kia. For a family with suitcases, the Hyundai carries more.

The EV6, on the other hand, is longer, lower (1,575 mm against 1,605 mm) and slightly narrower (1,880 mm against 1,890 mm).

### Warranty and equipment

The Kia has a clear lead here: a 7-year or 150,000 km (93,000 mile) general warranty. Hyundai gives 5 years, but with no mileage limit, which favors high-mileage drivers. The battery warranty is the same on both: 8 years or 160,000 km (99,000 miles).

In standard equipment they match on the basics: heated seats, Apple CarPlay, Android Auto, adaptive cruise control and automatic braking. Neither has a 360° camera, blind spot monitor or panoramic roof.

This comparison does not take price into account. The IONIQ 5 suits people who need trunk and cabin space, or who cover high mileage and want an unlimited warranty. The EV6 suits people who prefer a lower silhouette and value a warranty that lasts more years.
