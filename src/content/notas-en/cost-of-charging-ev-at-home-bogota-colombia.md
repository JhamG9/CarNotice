The question that comes up most on social media when someone gets a new EV is always the same: **how much will my electricity bill go up?** The short answer is much less than you would spend on gasoline, but the exact number depends on three things: your *estrato*, the Colombian socioeconomic tier that sets utility rates, how many kilometers you drive and whether you charge at home or on the street.

We did the math with Enel's real tariff for Bogotá.

## The tariff you pay in 2026

Enel's unit cost for Bogotá was **COP 863.6 per kWh in August 2026**. That is the full tariff paid by tier 4 households. The others are calculated from it:

| Tier | What you pay per kWh |
|---|---|
| 1, 2 and 3 | Subsidized tariff, but only on the first 130 kWh of the month |
| 4 | Full tariff: about COP 864 |
| 5 and 6 | Full tariff plus a 20% contribution: about COP 1,036 |

Here is the detail almost nobody explains: **the subsidy only covers subsistence consumption**, which in Bogotá is 130 kWh a month. An average household already uses much of that on the fridge, lights and washing machine. Everything the car adds above that cap is charged at the full tariff, even if the home is tier 2 or 3. In practice, charging an EV costs almost the same in tier 2 as in tier 4.

## How much energy an EV uses

A compact EV or a midsize SUV uses between 14 and 17 kWh per 100 km in the city. To that you must add AC charging losses, about 10%. For the calculation we use **16.5 kWh per 100 km measured at the household meter**.

## The math: 1,000 km (620 miles) a month

| Where you charge | Price per kWh | Cost of 1,000 km |
|---|---|---|
| Home, tier 4 | COP 864 | **COP 142,500** |
| Home, tier 6 | COP 1,036 | **COP 171,000** |
| Gasoline car at 40 km per gallon (25 mpg) | COP 16,331 per gallon in Bogotá | **COP 408,300** |

With those figures, a kilometer in an EV charged at home comes to about **COP 142**, against about **COP 408** in an efficient gasoline car. If the gasoline car gets 35 km per gallon (22 mpg), the gap widens: the month comes to COP 466,600.

## Three things that move the number

1. **A wall charger does not change the price per kWh.** It charges faster than the regular outlet cable and loses less energy, but the tariff is the same.
2. **Fast charging is the most expensive.** It is useful on the highway, but if it becomes routine, the saving against gasoline drops by half.
3. **The tariff changes every month.** Between March and August 2026, Enel's unit cost moved between COP 820 and COP 898 per kWh. It is not a fixed price like a cell phone plan.

> Before buying, check your bill: find how many kWh you use today and add what the car would use. That way you will know what part of the new consumption you will pay at the full tariff.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does a full charge at home cost?

It depends on battery size. A 60 kWh battery, charged from nearly empty, costs about COP 57,000 in tier 4, counting charging losses. Cars are rarely charged from zero to full, so the real cost is usually lower.

### Is charging cheaper in tier 2?

Very slightly. The subsidy applies only to the first 130 kWh of the month, and the car's consumption almost always falls above that limit.

### Do I need a separate meter for the car?

It is not mandatory in a house. In a residential complex it is usually required so that consumption is not charged to the common areas.

### Is there a cheaper night tariff in Colombia?

Not for most households. Enel mentions tariff options that require changing the metering system, and they must be arranged directly with the company.
