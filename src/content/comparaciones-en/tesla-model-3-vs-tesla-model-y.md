descripcion: "Tesla Model 3 vs Model Y, European entry specs: same 60 kWh battery, but the sedan goes 47 km further and the SUV has 241 liters more trunk space."
fecha: 2026-11-03
---

This comparison uses the European specs of the RWD versions of both cars, model year 2026. Since they share a battery, rear motor and fast charging, what separates them is the body: a sedan and an SUV, both midsize.

Both carry a 60 kWh LFP battery and accept up to 175 kW on DC, with 24 minutes to go from 10 to 80%. There is no difference there, nor in home charging, at 11 kW on both. Any advantage is decided by how much energy each one needs to move.

The Model 3 uses 12.2 kWh per 100 km and the Model Y, 13.4 kWh. With the same battery, that translates into 572 km (355 miles) WLTP for the sedan against 525 km (326 miles) for the SUV, a difference of 47 km (29 miles). The Model Y is taller (1,621 mm against 1,440 mm) and weighs 1,906 kg, 134 kg more than the Model 3's 1,772 kg, and both things cost energy on the highway. People who make long trips with few stops benefit from that extra distance.

The SUV makes up for it with space. Its rear trunk holds 835 liters against 594 for the sedan, and the front trunk goes from 88 to 117 liters. For a family with a stroller, bicycles or several days of luggage, 241 more liters in the main trunk count for more than 47 km (29 miles) of range. Both have five seats and ISOFIX.

On performance the spec sheets offer another surprise: the Model Y has more power (220 kW, or 295 hp, against 208 kW, or 279 hp), but the Model 3 accelerates better, from 0 to 100 km/h in 6.2 s against 7.2 s. The sedan's lower weight contributes to that difference. Both top out at 201 km/h.

On equipment they match with a 15.4-inch screen, heat pump, heated seats, wireless charger, autonomous emergency braking and 5 Euro NCAP stars, and neither offers Android Auto or Apple CarPlay. The Model Y's spec sheet says it has no lane keeping assist, while the Model 3 does. The warranty is the same: 4 years or 80,000 km (50,000 miles), with 8 years or 160,000 km (99,000 miles) for the battery.

The Model 3 suits people who prioritize range, efficiency and acceleration and do not need as much trunk space. The Model Y suits people who need generous cargo room or a higher driving position, and accept less distance per charge. This comparison does not take price into account, which could tip the balance either way.
