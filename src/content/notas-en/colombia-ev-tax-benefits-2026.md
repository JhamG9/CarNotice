EVs were **17.4% of the new cars registered in September 2026** in Colombia, and much of that jump is explained by incentives. But they do not all work the same way: some apply automatically on the dealer's invoice, and others depend on certificates, appointments with the DIAN, the country's tax authority, and how much income tax you pay.

This is the list, ordered from the most automatic to the most cumbersome.

## The ones that apply automatically

| Benefit | What it is | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 5% VAT | EVs pay 5% VAT, not the general 19% | Article 468-1 of the Tax Code |
| Zero tariff | EV imports pay no tariff | Foreign trade decrees |
| No consumption tax | EVs are excluded from the national consumption tax | Tax Code |

In practice, the reduced VAT is the biggest of them all. On a COP 100 million car, the difference between paying 5% and 19% on the same base is about **COP 13.3 million**.

## The ones that apply when you use the car

- **Vehicle tax:** it cannot exceed 1% of the commercial valuation. Each department or municipality can also give discounts. In Bogotá, new private EVs get a **60% discount for five years**, and hybrids 40%.
- **SOAT:** insurers must apply a **10% discount on the premium** of this mandatory accident insurance.
- **Technical inspection:** it has a discounted rate for EVs.
- **Pico y placa and car-free days:** Law 1964 of 2019 exempts EVs from the circulation restrictions set by local authorities, except those for security reasons.
- **Preferential parking:** the same law requires spaces to be reserved for EVs in certain parking lots.

## The ones that take paperwork

Here come the two benefits of **Law 1715 of 2014**, which the DIAN recognizes for individuals buying an EV or a hybrid for personal use, as long as they have a **certificate from the UPME**, the country's energy planning unit:

1. **Refund of the VAT paid.** Since the purchase is excluded from VAT, you can ask the DIAN to return the 5% paid on the invoice. On a COP 100 million car, that is about COP 4.76 million.
2. **Income tax deduction.** It allows you to deduct up to 50% of the car's value, spread over up to 15 years, with several caps.

Both can be requested on the same purchase, but the VAT refunded to you cannot also be used as a cost or income tax deduction. In separate articles we explain [how to request the VAT refund](/es/notas/devolucion-iva-carro-electrico-dian-paso-a-paso/) and [what the income tax deduction is really worth](/es/notas/deduccion-renta-carro-electrico-ley-1715/).

## What to know before counting on everything

- **The UPME certificate comes first.** Without it there is no refund or deduction. Requests grew so much in 2026 that the UPME had to speed up the process.
- **The income tax deduction does not work for everyone.** If you do not file income tax, or you already reach the deduction cap with your salary, the real benefit may be small.
- **Local discounts change by city.** Bogotá's 60% does not apply across the country.
- **The hybrid exemption from pico y placa has an end date.** In Bogotá, it runs until December 31, 2027. The EV exemption comes from a national law.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do hybrids have the same benefits?

Not all of them. Hybrids have smaller vehicle tax discounts, and in Bogotá their exemption from pico y placa expires at the end of 2027. For the VAT refund and income tax deduction, the DIAN includes HEV and PHEV hybrids with a UPME certificate.

### Are the 5% VAT and the refund the same thing?

No. The 5% is the rate paid when buying. The refund is a later procedure to recover that 5%, and it requires the UPME certificate.

### Do the benefits apply to used cars?

The reduced VAT and the refund apply to buying a new car. The vehicle tax capped at 1%, discounted SOAT and exemption from pico y placa do stay with the car when it changes owners.
