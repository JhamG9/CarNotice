This compares the versions sold in Colombia: the BYD Seagull GL 300, also known as the Dolphin Mini in other Latin American countries, and the Chevrolet Spark EUV in its single trim. Both are four-seaters, but the Spark EUV is a small SUV and the Seagull a hatchback.

Range is compared on NEDC, the cycle both spec sheets include: 300 km (186 miles) for the Seagull and 360 km (224 miles) for the Spark EUV. The difference comes from the battery, 41.9 kWh against 30.1 kWh. The Spark is also rated at 298 km (185 miles) WLTP, a tougher cycle, so its 360 km (224 miles) should be read with caution. For anyone covering less than 100 km (62 miles) a day, both are enough; the Spark gives more margin when leaving the city.

### Safety

This is the clearest difference. The Spark EUV has six airbags, autonomous emergency braking, adaptive cruise control, lane keeping and a 360° camera. On the Seagull's spec sheet those aids are listed as not standard and there are four airbags. That equipment matters most to people who drive with children or on the highway.

### Size, power and charging

The Seagull is 3,780 mm long and the Spark, 4,000 mm. The trunk goes from 230 to 355 liters: the difference between fitting the weekly shop or just a couple of bags. The Seagull delivers 55 kW (74 hp) and goes from 0 to 100 km/h in 13.9 s; the Spark, 74.6 kW (100 hp) and 11.2 s.

On DC, the Seagull accepts 30 kW, from 30 to 80% in 30 minutes, with a GB/T connector. The Spark accepts 50 kW and takes 35 minutes over the same stretch, with a CCS2 connector; with GB/T, you need to confirm that the station offers it. The Seagull has V2L, to power devices from the battery; the Spark's spec sheet has no V2L data.

### Who each one is for

The Seagull suits people who need the most compact city car and value V2L. The Spark EUV suits people who want more trunk space, more range and standard safety aids. This comparison does not take price into account.
