This compares the European specs of the Kia EV6 Standard Range 2WD and the Tesla Model Y RWD. They are not the same size: the EV6 is compact and the Model Y midsize, so many differences come from the scale of the car and not the brand.

The Model Y leads where distance is concerned. It is rated at 525 km (326 miles) WLTP against 428 km (266 miles) for the EV6, with a 60 kWh LFP battery against 63 kWh NMC. It uses 13.4 kWh per 100 km and the Kia 16.4 kWh, and that efficiency explains why it goes almost 100 km (62 miles) further with less stored energy. For intercity drivers, that means less time plugged in.

It is also more powerful: 220 kW (295 hp) and 420 Nm against 125 kW (168 hp) and 350 Nm, with 0 to 100 km/h in 7.2 s against 8.7 s. Both have rear-wheel drive and a single motor.

### Fast charging

The picture changes here. The EV6 accepts up to 195 kW on DC and goes from 10 to 80% in 18 minutes. The Model Y reaches 175 kW and needs 24 minutes. With a bigger battery but less range, the Kia does not win on distance, but it does win on how long the stop lasts. Both charge at 11 kW on AC.

### Trunk and size

The Model Y is 4,794 mm long and 1,982 mm wide; the EV6, 4,695 mm and 1,880 mm. That difference shows in the trunk: 835 liters plus 117 under the hood, against 490 and 52 for the Kia. For a family traveling with luggage, the Tesla solves the problem without accessories. For anyone parking on narrow streets, the EV6 is easier to handle.

### Equipment and warranty

The Model Y has a 15.4-inch screen, a wireless charger and a heat pump, but offers neither Apple CarPlay nor Android Auto. The EV6 has both, with a 12.3-inch screen, which makes it more convenient for people who rely on their phone on the dashboard. The general warranty also counts: 7 years or 150,000 km (93,000 miles) on the Kia, 4 years or 80,000 km (50,000 miles) on the Tesla. On the battery they match: 8 years and 160,000 km (99,000 miles).

This comparison does not take price into account. The Model Y suits people who prioritize range, power and trunk space. The EV6 suits people who want shorter charging stops, phone integration and more years of warranty.
