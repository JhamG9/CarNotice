---

This compares the European specs of the Hyundai IONIQ 5 63 kWh RWD and the Tesla Model Y RWD, model year 2026. They are rear-wheel-drive, five-seat electric crossovers, although the spec sheets classify the IONIQ 5 as compact and the Model Y as midsize, and that shows in almost every figure.

The dimensions make it clear. The Hyundai is 4,655 mm long and the Tesla 4,794 mm, but the IONIQ 5's wheelbase is longer: 3,000 mm against 2,890 mm. In other words, a shorter car with the cabin stretched toward the wheels, easier to drive and park in the city without giving up passenger space.

The trunk is where the difference flips. The Model Y offers 835 liters and 117 more in the front trunk; the IONIQ 5, 520 and 57. For anyone carrying strollers, suitcases or sports gear, the Tesla has 315 liters more main space.

On range and consumption, the Model Y also leads: 525 km (326 miles) WLTP against 440 km (273 miles), with 13.4 kWh per 100 km against 15.6 kWh. The Hyundai has a 63 kWh NMC battery, slightly larger than the Tesla's 60 kWh LFP, but it uses more energy per kilometer. On the highway, 85 km (53 miles) less range means planning a stop sooner.

That stop, though, is shorter with the Hyundai. It accepts 195 kW and goes from 10 to 80% in 18 minutes; the Tesla accepts 175 kW and takes 24. People who mix urban driving with the occasional long trip may prefer charging quickly several times to having more distance per charge.

On power, the Model Y delivers 220 kW (295 hp) and the IONIQ 5, 125 kW (168 hp). That translates into 7.2 s against 8.5 s from 0 to 100 km/h and a top speed of 201 km/h against 185 km/h.

On equipment, the Hyundai adds Android Auto, Apple CarPlay and V2L, absent from the Tesla's spec sheet, while the Model Y has a heat pump and the IONIQ 5 does not. Neither records ventilated seats or a panoramic roof. The Hyundai's warranty is 5 years with no mileage limit; the Tesla's, 4 years or 80,000 km (50,000 miles).

The IONIQ 5 suits people who drive mainly in the city and want a short car with good interior space, fast charging and CarPlay. The Model Y suits people who need a big trunk, more range and more power. This comparison does not take price into account.
